# 01 · Project vision

## Experience promise

A personal developer portfolio imagined as a private racing workshop at blue hour: precise engineering, warm practical light, a purposeful single-seat car, and clear evidence of the developer's work. Visitors should feel invited into a working studio, not trapped inside a game trailer.

Brand voice: **mechanical, composed, human**. Visual register: brand portfolio. Physical references: a clean endurance-racing workshop, brushed tool drawers, painted pit markings, and overhead photographic softboxes. Avoid copying official F1 broadcast graphics or a team's livery.

## Audience and jobs

| Audience | Primary question | Fastest path |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter | Who is this and are they relevant? | Hero → featured project → résumé/contact |
| Engineering lead | Can they explain technical decisions? | Projects → case study → source/demo |
| Collaborator | What can we build together? | Skills → about → contact |
| Design/3D enthusiast | How was this experience built? | Optional technical case study |

Proposed usability targets: a first-time visitor can identify name and role within 10 seconds, open a relevant case study within 20 seconds, and find contact without finishing the journey. Validate with at least five representative people; these are targets, not claims.

## Content-to-world mapping

| Portfolio meaning | World element | Plain-language label |
|---|---|---|
| Personal identity | Car and helmet in starting bay | Name and role |
| Projects | Three adjacent garage bays | Projects |
| Skills | Engineering bench and diagnostic panel | Skills |
| Achievements | Small illuminated display shelf | Achievements |
| Biography and experience | Driver dossier and workshop desk | About / Experience |
| Collaboration | Pit exit into open daylight | Contact |

Motorsport labels are secondary captions. A visitor need not understand racing terminology.

## Version 1 scope

One landing route; three strong case-study routes; one generic formula-style car; one modular garage; six narrative chapters; a portrait or helmet; a concise experience list; verified achievements; résumé download; direct contact links. A desktop canvas enhances the landing page. Case studies remain conventional reading pages.

Default exclusions: driving physics, multiplayer, free-roam navigation, photorealistic crowds, complex character rigging, live race data, a CMS, login, autoplay audio, and custom cursors. A contact form, soundscape, and explore mode require separate post-launch decisions.

## Design principles

1. Evidence leads: project outcomes receive more space than technology logos.
2. Navigation stays familiar: native scroll, visible links, working Back button.
3. Materials behave plausibly: stable light, measured roughness, restrained reflections.
4. Motion has a destination: each move reveals content or explains location.
5. Failure preserves the portfolio: the canvas is optional and independently recoverable.

## Acceptance

The experience succeeds when it feels distinctive with the canvas enabled and complete with it disabled. Premium quality comes from consistent spacing, sharp imagery, believable lighting, and well-edited content. The 3D layer must earn its cost in attention, bandwidth, and maintenance.
